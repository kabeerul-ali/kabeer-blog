India's Neighbouring Countries

1. Pakistan

Pakistan is a country in South Asia, bordered by India, China, Afghanistan, and Iran, with a coastline along the Arabian Sea. Its capital city is Islamabad, while Karachi is its largest city and an important economic centre. Pakistan became independent on 14 August 1947. The country has diverse landscapes, including the Himalayan and Karakoram mountains, fertile plains, deserts, forests, and coastal regions. K2, the world's second-highest mountain, lies in the Karakoram Range in Pakistan. The Indus River is central to the country's agriculture and supports millions of people. Pakistan has a rich cultural heritage influenced by ancient civilizations, including the Indus Valley Civilization. Urdu is the national language, while English and several regional languages are also used. The country is known for its traditional food, music, historical sites, and mountain scenery.

  • Capital: Islamabad
  • Location: South Asia
  • Area: About 881,913 km²
  • Population: More than 240 million (2023 census reported population)
  • Official Language: Urdu and English used for official purposes
  • Currency: Pakistani Rupee (PKR)
  • Highest Peak: K2 (8,611 metres)
  • Special Fact: Pakistan is home to K2, the world's second-highest mountain.

2. China

China is a country in East Asia and one of the world's oldest continuous civilizations. Its capital is Beijing, while Shanghai is one of its largest economic and financial centres. China shares a border with 14 countries, including India, Pakistan, Nepal, and Bhutan. The country has diverse landscapes, ranging from the Himalayas and the Tibetan Plateau to vast deserts, fertile plains, and long coastlines. China is famous for the Great Wall, the Forbidden City, and the Terracotta Army. The Yangtze is the longest river in Asia, flowing through China. Chinese culture has made important contributions to paper, printing, and other inventions. Today, China is a major global centre for manufacturing, technology, trade, and infrastructure. It also faces challenges such as air pollution, an ageing population, and environmental protection.

  • Capital: Beijing
  • Location: East Asia
  • Area: About 9.6 million km²
  • Population: More than 1.4 billion people
  • Official Language: Standard Chinese (Mandarin)
  • Currency: Renminbi (Yuan)
  • Highest Peak: Mount Everest (shared border with Nepal)
  • Special Fact: The Great Wall is one of China's most famous historical landmarks.

3. Nepal

Nepal is a landlocked country in South Asia, located between India and China. Its capital and largest city is Kathmandu. Nepal is famous for the Himalayan mountain range, including Mount Everest, the highest mountain in the world. The country has diverse landscapes, from the snow-covered Himalayas in the north to green hills, valleys, and fertile plains in the south. Nepal has a rich cultural heritage influenced by Hinduism and Buddhism, with ancient temples, monasteries, festivals, and traditional architecture. The birthplace of Gautama Buddha is traditionally identified as Lumbini, in southern Nepal. Tourism, agriculture, and hydropower are important parts of its economy. Nepal is also known for its national parks and wildlife, including the Bengal tiger and one-horned rhinoceros. Despite its natural beauty, the country faces challenges such as earthquakes, difficult mountain terrain, and limited infrastructure in remote areas.

  • Capital: Kathmandu
  • Location: South Asia
  • Area: About 147,516 km²
  • Population: Around 30 million people
  • Official Language: Nepali
  • Currency: Nepalese Rupee (NPR)
  • Highest Peak: Mount Everest (8,848.86 metres)
  • Special Fact: Nepal is home to eight of the world's fourteen mountains higher than 8,000 metres.

4. Bhutan

Bhutan is a small, landlocked country in South Asia, located in the Eastern Himalayas between India and China. Its capital city is Thimphu. Bhutan is famous for its beautiful mountains, green valleys, Buddhist monasteries, and traditional culture. The country is home to dramatic Himalayan landscapes, forests, rivers, and rare wildlife. Tiger's Nest Monastery, also known as Paro Taktsang, is one of its most famous landmarks, built on a steep cliff above the Paro Valley. Bhutan follows the philosophy of Gross National Happiness, which emphasizes well-being alongside economic development. Buddhism plays an important role in its culture, festivals, architecture, and daily life. Hydropower, agriculture, and tourism are important parts of its economy. Bhutan is also known for protecting its forests and natural environment, although climate change and melting Himalayan glaciers remain important challenges.

  • Capital: Thimphu
  • Location: Eastern Himalayas, South Asia
  • Area: About 38,394 km²
  • Population: Around 800,000 people
  • Official Language: Dzongkha
  • Currency: Bhutanese Ngultrum (BTN)
  • Highest Peak: Gangkhar Puensum (commonly cited at about 7,570 metres)
  • Special Fact: Bhutan is known for its Gross National Happiness philosophy.

5. Bangladesh

Bangladesh is a country in South Asia, bordered by India on most sides, Myanmar to the southeast, and the Bay of Bengal to the south. Its capital and largest city is Dhaka. Bangladesh is known for its numerous rivers, fertile delta plains, green countryside, and rich cultural heritage. The Ganges, Brahmaputra, and Meghna river systems shape much of the country's landscape. The Sundarbans, shared with India, is the world's largest mangrove forest and is home to the Bengal tiger. Bangladesh became an independent country in 1971 after the Bangladesh Liberation War. Bengali is the official language, and the country celebrates its language, literature, music, and traditional festivals. Textiles and garment manufacturing are important to its economy, along with agriculture and remittances. Bangladesh is also vulnerable to floods, cyclones, and rising sea levels because much of its land is low-lying.

  • Capital: Dhaka
  • Location: South Asia
  • Area: About 148,460 km²
  • Population: More than 170 million people
  • Official Language: Bengali (Bangla)
  • Currency: Bangladeshi Taka (BDT)
  • Highest Point: Saka Haphong (commonly cited at about 1,064 metres)
  • Special Fact: Bangladesh is home to the world's largest mangrove forest, the Sundarbans, shared with India.

6. Myanmar

Myanmar, also known as Burma, is a country in Southeast Asia bordered by India, Bangladesh, China, Laos, and Thailand. Its capital is Naypyidaw, while Yangon is its largest city. Myanmar is known for its ancient temples, Buddhist monasteries, mountains, rivers, and tropical landscapes. Bagan is one of its most famous historical sites, featuring thousands of ancient temples and pagodas across a vast plain. The Irrawaddy River is the country's most important river and supports agriculture and transportation. Myanmar has diverse ethnic communities, languages, traditional arts, and cultural festivals. Agriculture plays an important role in its economy, with rice among its major crops. The country also has valuable natural resources and beautiful coastal areas along the Bay of Bengal and the Andaman Sea. Myanmar has faced significant political conflict and humanitarian challenges in recent years.

  • Capital: Naypyidaw
  • Location: Southeast Asia
  • Area: About 676,578 km²
  • Population: Approximately 55 million people
  • Official Language: Burmese
  • Currency: Myanmar Kyat (MMK)
  • Highest Peak: Hkakabo Razi (about 5,881 metres)
  • Special Fact: Bagan is famous for its thousands of ancient temples and pagodas.

7. Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka is an island country in South Asia, located in the Indian Ocean, southeast of India. Its legislative capital is Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte, while Colombo is its largest city and a major commercial centre. Sri Lanka is famous for its golden beaches, tropical forests, tea plantations, ancient temples, and rich cultural heritage. The country has a long history influenced by Buddhism, and historic sites such as Sigiriya, Anuradhapura, and Polonnaruwa attract visitors from around the world. Sri Lanka is also known for its tea production, spices, rice cultivation, and diverse wildlife, including elephants and leopards. Its central highlands contain scenic mountains and waterfalls, while its coastline supports fishing and tourism. Sri Lanka gained independence from British rule in 1948. The country continues to develop its economy while protecting its natural environment and cultural heritage.

  • Capital: Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte
  • Location: South Asia, Indian Ocean
  • Area: About 65,610 km²
  • Population: Around 22 million people
  • Official Languages: Sinhala and Tamil
  • Currency: Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR)
  • Highest Peak: Pidurutalagala (2,524 metres)
  • Special Fact: Sri Lanka is internationally famous for its Ceylon tea.

8. Maldives

Maldives is an island country in South Asia, located in the Indian Ocean southwest of India and Sri Lanka. Its capital and largest city is Malé. The country consists of around 1,190 coral islands grouped into 26 natural atolls, although only a portion of these islands are inhabited. Maldives is famous for its white sandy beaches, turquoise lagoons, coral reefs, and colourful marine life. Tourism is one of its most important industries, attracting visitors for snorkelling, scuba diving, and luxury island resorts. Fishing is another traditional part of its economy. The official language is Dhivehi, and Islam is the state religion. Because most of its islands are very low-lying, Maldives is especially vulnerable to rising sea levels and coastal erosion. Protecting its coral reefs and marine ecosystems is essential for the country's future.

  • Capital: Malé
  • Location: Indian Ocean, South Asia
  • Land Area: About 300 km²
  • Population: Around 500,000 people
  • Official Language: Dhivehi
  • Currency: Maldivian Rufiyaa (MVR)
  • Highest Natural Point: Approximately 5.1 metres above sea level
  • Special Fact: Maldives is one of the world's lowest-lying countries.

9. Afghanistan

Afghanistan is a landlocked country in South-Central Asia, bordered by Pakistan, Iran, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, and China. Its capital and largest city is Kabul. The country is known for its rugged mountains, deep valleys, deserts, and historic trade routes that once connected Central Asia with South Asia and the Middle East. The Hindu Kush mountain range stretches across much of Afghanistan and shapes its climate and landscape. Afghanistan has a rich cultural heritage, influenced by Persian, Central Asian, and South Asian traditions. Dari and Pashto are its official languages. Agriculture, including the cultivation of wheat, fruits, and nuts, supports many communities. The country is also famous for historical sites such as the Minaret of Jam and the ancient city of Herat. Afghanistan has faced decades of conflict, economic hardship, and humanitarian challenges, which continue to affect many people.

  • Capital: Kabul
  • Location: South-Central Asia
  • Area: About 652,230 km²
  • Population: Around 40–45 million people (estimates vary)
  • Official Languages: Dari and Pashto
  • Currency: Afghan Afghani (AFN)
  • Highest Peak: Noshaq (about 7,492 metres)
  • Special Fact: Afghanistan's Hindu Kush mountains form part of the western Himalayan mountain region.
