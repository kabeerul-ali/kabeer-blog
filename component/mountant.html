1. Mount Everest

Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world, located in the Himalayas on the border between Nepal and China (Tibet). It stands at approximately 8,848.86 metres (29,031.7 feet) above sea level. Everest is part of the Mahalangur Himal range and was formed by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates. The mountain is famous for its snow-covered peaks, extreme weather conditions, thin air, and challenging climbing routes. It was first successfully climbed on 29 May 1953 by Sir Edmund Hillary of New Zealand and Tenzing Norgay Sherpa of Nepal. Every year, climbers from around the world attempt to reach its summit. Mount Everest remains a symbol of adventure, courage, determination, and natural beauty.

  • Location: Nepal–China (Tibet) border
  • Height: 8,848.86 metres (29,031.7 feet)
  • Mountain Range: Mahalangur Himal (Himalayas)
  • Type: Himalayan mountain
  • First Successful Ascent: 29 May 1953
  • First Climbers: Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay
  • Famous For: Being the highest mountain in the world
  • Special Fact: Everest was named after Sir George Everest, a former Surveyor General of India.

2. K2

K2 is the second-highest mountain in the world, standing at approximately 8,611 metres (28,251 feet) above sea level. It is located in the Karakoram Range on the border between Pakistan and China. K2 is famous for its steep slopes, unpredictable weather, and extremely difficult climbing conditions. It is often called the "Savage Mountain" because of the serious dangers faced by climbers attempting to reach its summit. Unlike Mount Everest, K2 is considered especially challenging because of its technical climbing routes and frequent severe storms. The mountain was first successfully climbed on 31 July 1954 by an Italian expedition led by Ardito Desio, with Achille Compagnoni and Lino Lacedelli reaching the summit. K2 is one of the most impressive and challenging peaks on Earth.

  • Location: Pakistan–China border
  • Height: 8,611 metres (28,251 feet)
  • Mountain Range: Karakoram
  • Type: High-altitude mountain peak
  • First Successful Ascent: 31 July 1954
  • First Climbers: Achille Compagnoni and Lino Lacedelli
  • Famous For: Its extreme climbing difficulty
  • Special Fact: K2 is the highest mountain in the Karakoram Range.

3. Kangchenjunga

Kangchenjunga is the third-highest mountain in the world, rising to approximately 8,586 metres (28,169 feet) above sea level. It is located on the border between Nepal and the Indian state of Sikkim, in the eastern Himalayas. Its name is often interpreted as "The Five Treasures of the Great Snow," referring to its five major peaks. Kangchenjunga is known for its enormous glaciers, rugged ridges, deep valleys, and breathtaking snow-covered scenery. The mountain holds deep cultural and religious significance for local communities, particularly in Sikkim. Its first successful ascent took place on 25 May 1955 by Joe Brown and George Band, members of a British expedition. They stopped just short of the summit out of respect for a local tradition. Kangchenjunga remains one of the most challenging and spectacular mountains in the Himalayas.

  • Location: Nepal–Sikkim, India border
  • Height: 8,586 metres (28,169 feet)
  • Mountain Range: Himalayas
  • Type: Himalayan mountain peak
  • First Successful Ascent: 25 May 1955
  • First Climbers: Joe Brown and George Band
  • Famous For: Being the world's third-highest mountain
  • Special Fact: Its name refers to the five treasures of the great snow.

4. Lhotse

Lhotse is the fourth-highest mountain in the world, standing at approximately 8,516 metres (27,940 feet) above sea level. It is located in the Himalayas on the border between Nepal and China (Tibet), very close to Mount Everest. Lhotse's name means "South Peak" in Tibetan, reflecting its position south of Everest. The mountain is famous for its steep faces, sharp ridges, icy slopes, and challenging climbing routes. It shares part of its climbing route with Mount Everest before separating toward its own summit. Lhotse was first successfully climbed on 18 May 1956 by Swiss climbers Ernst Reiss and Fritz Luchsinger. Its enormous south face is one of the most impressive and difficult mountain walls in the world. Lhotse remains a remarkable symbol of the beauty and challenges of high-altitude mountaineering.

  • Location: Nepal–China (Tibet) border
  • Height: 8,516 metres (27,940 feet)
  • Mountain Range: Himalayas
  • Type: Himalayan mountain peak
  • First Successful Ascent: 18 May 1956
  • First Climbers: Ernst Reiss and Fritz Luchsinger
  • Famous For: Its enormous and steep south face
  • Special Fact: Lhotse is connected to the Mount Everest massif.

5. Makalu

Makalu is the fifth-highest mountain in the world, standing at approximately 8,485 metres (27,838 feet) above sea level. It is located in the Mahalangur Himalayas on the border between Nepal and China (Tibet), southeast of Mount Everest. Makalu is famous for its pyramid-shaped summit, steep ridges, and challenging climbing routes. Its exposed slopes and unpredictable weather make climbing particularly difficult. The mountain is surrounded by glaciers, rugged valleys, and spectacular Himalayan scenery. Makalu was first successfully climbed on 15 May 1955 by Lionel Terray and Jean Couzy, members of a French expedition. The mountain is part of Makalu-Barun National Park in Nepal, an area known for its rich biodiversity and dramatic landscapes. Makalu is admired by mountaineers for both its natural beauty and its technical climbing challenges.

  • Location: Nepal–China (Tibet) border
  • Height: 8,485 metres (27,838 feet)
  • Mountain Range: Mahalangur Himal, Himalayas
  • Type: Himalayan mountain peak
  • First Successful Ascent: 15 May 1955
  • First Climbers: Lionel Terray and Jean Couzy
  • Famous For: Its distinctive pyramid-shaped summit
  • Special Fact: Makalu is known for its steep, exposed climbing routes.

6. Cho Oyu

Cho Oyu is the sixth-highest mountain in the world, standing at approximately 8,188 metres (26,864 feet) above sea level. It is located in the Himalayas on the border between Nepal and China (Tibet), west of Mount Everest. Its name means "Turquoise Goddess" in Tibetan. Cho Oyu is known for its beautiful snow-covered slopes, enormous glaciers, and impressive Himalayan scenery. Compared with several other mountains above 8,000 metres, it is often considered a more accessible climbing peak because some routes have less technical difficulty, although climbing remains dangerous due to extreme altitude and severe weather. Cho Oyu was first successfully climbed on 19 October 1954 by an Austrian expedition involving Herbert Tichy, Joseph Jöchler, and Sherpa Pasang Dawa Lama. The mountain attracts experienced climbers from around the world and is an important part of the Himalayan landscape.

  • Location: Nepal–China (Tibet) border
  • Height: 8,188 metres (26,864 feet)
  • Mountain Range: Himalayas
  • Type: Himalayan mountain peak
  • First Successful Ascent: 19 October 1954
  • First Climbers: Herbert Tichy, Joseph Jöchler, and Pasang Dawa Lama
  • Famous For: Being considered one of the more accessible 8,000-metre peaks
  • Special Fact: Its name means "Turquoise Goddess" in Tibetan.

7. Dhaulagiri I

Dhaulagiri I is the seventh-highest mountain in the world, standing at approximately 8,167 metres (26,795 feet) above sea level. It is located entirely within Nepal in the western Himalayas. Its name comes from Sanskrit words meaning "White Mountain," describing its magnificent snow-covered slopes. Dhaulagiri I is famous for its massive summit, steep ridges, glaciers, and dramatic mountain scenery. Its challenging terrain, avalanches, and unpredictable weather make climbing dangerous. The mountain was first successfully climbed on 13 May 1960 by a Swiss-Austrian-Nepali expedition. Dhaulagiri I was once considered the highest mountain in the world before more accurate measurements identified taller peaks. Today, it remains one of Nepal's most impressive Himalayan mountains and attracts experienced mountaineers from around the world.

  • Location: Nepal
  • Height: 8,167 metres (26,795 feet)
  • Mountain Range: Himalayas
  • Type: Himalayan mountain peak
  • First Successful Ascent: 13 May 1960
  • Country: Nepal
  • Famous For: Its enormous snow-covered summit
  • Special Fact: Its name means "White Mountain."
