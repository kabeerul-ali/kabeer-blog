Mercury

Mercury is the smallest planet in our Solar System and the closest planet to the Sun. It has a rocky surface covered with thousands of impact craters. Mercury has almost no atmosphere to retain heat, causing extreme temperatures between day and night. It takes only 88 Earth days to complete one orbit around the Sun, making it the planet with the shortest year. Mercury has no natural moons. Interestingly, despite being closest to the Sun, some permanently shadowed craters near its poles contain water ice. Scientists study Mercury to understand how rocky planets form and evolve.

  • Position: 1st planet from the Sun
  • Diameter: About 4,879 km
  • Distance from Sun: 57.9 million km (average)
  • Gravity: 3.7 m/s²
  • Orbital Period: 88 Earth days
  • Rotation Period: About 59 Earth days
  • Natural Moons: 0
  • Special Fact: It has the shortest year of all eight planets.

Venus

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and is similar in size to Earth, which is why it is often called Earth's twin. However, its environment is extremely different. Venus has a thick atmosphere mainly made of carbon dioxide, which traps heat through a powerful greenhouse effect. This makes Venus the hottest planet in the Solar System, with an average surface temperature of about 465°C. Its surface contains mountains, volcanic plains, and impact craters. Venus rotates very slowly and in the opposite direction to most planets. It has no natural moons and is one of the brightest objects visible in Earth's sky.

  • Position: 2nd planet from the Sun
  • Diameter: About 12,104 km
  • Distance from Sun: 108.2 million km (average)
  • Gravity: 8.87 m/s²
  • Orbital Period: About 225 Earth days
  • Rotation Period: About 243 Earth days
  • Natural Moons: 0
  • Special Fact: Venus is the hottest planet in the Solar System.

Earth

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known planet to support life. It has a unique environment with liquid water, oxygen, and a protective atmosphere that makes life possible. About 71% of Earth's surface is covered by water, while the remaining area consists of continents and islands. Earth has a solid rocky surface, mountains, oceans, forests, and deserts. Its atmosphere protects living organisms from harmful solar radiation and helps regulate temperature. Earth takes about 365.25 days to complete one orbit around the Sun and rotates once approximately every 24 hours. It has one natural satellite, the Moon. Scientists continue to study Earth to understand its climate, ecosystems, and the importance of protecting our planet.

  • Position: 3rd planet from the Sun
  • Diameter: About 12,742 km
  • Distance from Sun: 149.6 million km (average)
  • Gravity: 9.81 m/s²
  • Orbital Period: About 365.25 days
  • Rotation Period: About 24 hours
  • Natural Moons: 1 (the Moon)
  • Special Fact: Earth is the only planet currently known to support life.

Mars

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and is often called the Red Planet because iron minerals in its soil give the surface a reddish appearance. It is a cold, rocky world with a thin atmosphere mainly composed of carbon dioxide. Mars has enormous volcanoes, deep valleys, ancient river channels, and polar ice caps. Olympus Mons, located on Mars, is the tallest known volcano in the Solar System. Scientists believe that liquid water may have flowed across its surface billions of years ago. Today, robotic rovers and orbiters explore Mars to study its geology and search for signs of ancient microbial life. Mars has two small natural moons, Phobos and Deimos.

  • Position: 4th planet from the Sun
  • Diameter: About 6,779 km
  • Distance from Sun: 227.9 million km (average)
  • Gravity: 3.71 m/s²
  • Orbital Period: About 687 Earth days
  • Rotation Period: About 24 hours 37 minutes
  • Natural Moons: 2 (Phobos and Deimos)
  • Special Fact: Olympus Mons on Mars is the tallest known volcano in the Solar System.

Jupiter

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest planet in our Solar System. It is a gas giant mainly composed of hydrogen and helium, with no solid surface like Earth. Jupiter is famous for its Great Red Spot, a gigantic storm that has lasted for centuries. Its atmosphere contains beautiful bands of clouds, powerful winds, and enormous storms. Jupiter has a very strong magnetic field and a faint ring system. It also has many natural moons, including Ganymede, the largest moon in the Solar System. Some of Jupiter's moons may contain underground oceans, making them interesting places for scientific research. Jupiter takes about 12 Earth years to complete one orbit around the Sun.

  • Position: 5th planet from the Sun
  • Diameter: About 139,820 km
  • Distance from Sun: 778.5 million km (average)
  • Gravity: About 24.79 m/s²
  • Orbital Period: About 11.86 Earth years
  • Rotation Period: About 9 hours 56 minutes
  • Natural Moons: 101 officially recognized
  • Special Fact: Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System.

Saturn

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest planet in our Solar System. It is a gas giant mainly composed of hydrogen and helium. Saturn is famous for its spectacular ring system, which consists mostly of ice particles, rocky material, and dust. Although other planets also have rings, Saturn's rings are the brightest and most visible. Saturn has a thick atmosphere with powerful winds and large storms. It is so lightweight for its size that its average density is lower than that of water. Saturn has many natural moons, including Titan, which has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane and ethane. Saturn takes about 29.5 Earth years to complete one orbit around the Sun.

  • Position: 6th planet from the Sun
  • Diameter: About 116,460 km
  • Distance from Sun: 1.43 billion km (average)
  • Gravity: About 10.44 m/s²
  • Orbital Period: About 29.5 Earth years
  • Rotation Period: About 10.7 hours
  • Natural Moons: 274 officially recognized
  • Special Fact: Saturn's beautiful rings are made mostly of ice and rocky particles.

Uranus

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and is known for its beautiful blue-green color. It is an ice giant mainly composed of hydrogen, helium, and materials such as water, ammonia, and methane. Methane in its atmosphere absorbs red light, giving Uranus its distinctive cyan appearance. Uranus has a faint ring system and many natural moons. One of its most unusual features is its extreme axial tilt of about 98 degrees, which makes it appear to rotate on its side. This unusual tilt causes extreme seasonal changes, with each pole experiencing long periods of sunlight and darkness. Uranus is extremely cold, and its atmosphere contains powerful winds. It takes about 84 Earth years to complete one orbit around the Sun.

  • Position: 7th planet from the Sun
  • Diameter: About 50,724 km
  • Distance from Sun: 2.87 billion km (average)
  • Gravity: About 8.69 m/s²
  • Orbital Period: About 84 Earth years
  • Rotation Period: About 17 hours 14 minutes
  • Natural Moons: 29 officially recognized
  • Special Fact: Uranus rotates almost on its side because of its extreme axial tilt.

Neptune

Neptune is the eighth and farthest planet from the Sun in our Solar System. It is an ice giant known for its deep blue color and extremely powerful winds. Its atmosphere mainly consists of hydrogen, helium, and methane, which contributes to its blue appearance. Neptune has a cold atmosphere, faint rings, and several natural moons. Its largest moon, Triton, moves around the planet in the opposite direction to Neptune's rotation. Neptune receives very little sunlight because of its enormous distance from the Sun. Despite this, it has a dynamic atmosphere with enormous storms and winds that can exceed 2,000 km/h. Neptune takes approximately 165 Earth years to complete one revolution around the Sun. Scientists study this distant planet to understand ice giants and the formation of planetary systems.

  • Position: 8th planet from the Sun
  • Diameter: About 49,244 km
  • Distance from Sun: About 4.5 billion km (average)
  • Gravity: About 11.15 m/s²
  • Orbital Period: About 165 Earth years
  • Rotation Period: About 16 hours
  • Natural Moons: 16
  • Special Fact: Neptune has some of the fastest winds recorded in the Solar System.
