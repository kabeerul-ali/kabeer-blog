Word's 7 Wonders

1. Great Wall of China

The Great Wall of China is one of the most famous historical structures in the world. It is located in China and was built over many centuries to protect Chinese states and empires from invasions and raids. Different sections were constructed by different dynasties, with major construction taking place during the Ming Dynasty. The wall crosses mountains, deserts, grasslands, and valleys. It includes walls, watchtowers, fortresses, and defensive passes. Its total length, including branches and associated defensive sections, is estimated at more than 21,000 kilometres. The Great Wall represents China's rich history, engineering skills, and cultural heritage. Today, it is a major tourist attraction and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

  • Location: China
  • Type: Ancient defensive fortification
  • Total Length: More than 21,000 km, including branches
  • Construction: Built and rebuilt over many centuries
  • Major Dynasty: Ming Dynasty
  • Purpose: Defence against invasions and raids
  • UNESCO Status: World Heritage Site since 1987
  • Special Fact: It crosses mountains, deserts, and valleys across northern China.

2. Taj Mahal

The Taj Mahal is one of the most beautiful historical monuments in the world. It is located in Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India, on the banks of the Yamuna River. Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan commissioned it in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal. Construction began around 1632 and the main mausoleum was completed around 1648, with other parts of the complex finished later. The monument is made primarily of white marble and decorated with intricate carvings, precious-stone inlay, and beautiful Islamic calligraphy. Its design combines Persian, Islamic, and Indian architectural influences. The Taj Mahal is famous for its symmetrical gardens, reflecting pools, grand dome, and four minarets. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a symbol of love and India's rich cultural heritage.

  • Location: Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India
  • Built By: Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan
  • Construction Began: Around 1632
  • Material: Mainly white marble
  • Purpose: Mausoleum for Mumtaz Mahal
  • Architectural Style: Mughal architecture
  • UNESCO Status: World Heritage Site since 1983
  • Special Fact: The monument's appearance can change with different lighting throughout the day.

3. Petra

Petra is an ancient archaeological city located in southern Jordan. It is famous for its magnificent buildings carved directly into rose-red sandstone cliffs, which earned it the nickname "Rose City." Petra was an important trading centre of the Nabataean Kingdom, especially around the first century AD. Its people developed impressive water management systems, including channels, dams, and reservoirs, allowing them to survive in a dry desert environment. The most famous monument is Al-Khazneh, also known as the Treasury, with its beautifully carved facade. Petra also contains tombs, temples, caves, and an ancient theatre. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985 and was named one of the New Seven Wonders of the World in 2007.

  • Location: Southern Jordan
  • Nickname: Rose City
  • Ancient People: Nabataeans
  • Famous Monument: Al-Khazneh (The Treasury)
  • Historical Role: Major trading centre
  • Architecture: Buildings carved into sandstone cliffs
  • UNESCO Status: World Heritage Site since 1985
  • Special Fact: Petra's ancient water systems helped people live in the desert.

4. Christ the Redeemer

Christ the Redeemer is a famous statue of Jesus Christ located on Mount Corcovado in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. It stands with outstretched arms overlooking the city and the Atlantic Ocean. The statue was completed in 1931 and has become one of the most recognizable landmarks in the world. It is made of reinforced concrete and covered with thousands of triangular soapstone tiles. The monument represents peace, faith, and welcome. Standing high above Rio de Janeiro, it offers spectacular views of the surrounding mountains, beaches, and city. The statue was designed by Brazilian engineer Heitor da Silva Costa, with contributions from French sculptor Paul Landowski. In 2007, Christ the Redeemer was named one of the New Seven Wonders of the World.

  • Location: Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
  • Height: About 30 metres, excluding the pedestal
  • Completed: 1931
  • Material: Reinforced concrete and soapstone
  • Architect: Heitor da Silva Costa
  • Mountain: Mount Corcovado
  • Symbolism: Peace, faith, and welcome
  • Special Fact: Its outstretched arms span about 28 metres.

5. Machu Picchu

Machu Picchu is an ancient Inca city located high in the Andes Mountains of Peru, South America. It stands at approximately 2,430 metres above sea level and is surrounded by green mountains and deep valleys. Built during the 15th century, it is believed to have been an important royal estate or ceremonial centre of the Inca civilization. The site is famous for its impressive stone buildings, agricultural terraces, temples, and carefully constructed stairways. Inca builders fitted massive stones together with remarkable precision, often without using mortar. Machu Picchu remained largely unknown to the outside world until it was brought to international attention in 1911. Today, it is one of Peru's most important archaeological sites and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

  • Location: Cusco Region, Peru
  • Built By: Inca civilization
  • Construction Period: 15th century
  • Elevation: About 2,430 metres
  • Famous For: Ancient stone architecture
  • Architectural Feature: Agricultural terraces
  • UNESCO Status: World Heritage Site since 1983
  • Special Fact: Many stone blocks were fitted together without mortar.

6. Chichén Itzá

Chichén Itzá is an ancient Maya city located in the Yucatán Peninsula of Mexico. It was an important political, religious, and cultural centre of the Maya civilization. The site is famous for El Castillo, also known as the Temple of Kukulcán, a massive stepped pyramid dedicated to the Maya feathered-serpent deity. The city flourished particularly between the ninth and twelfth centuries. Its architecture reflects the Maya people's advanced knowledge of mathematics, astronomy, and engineering. Other important structures include the Great Ball Court, the Temple of the Warriors, and the Sacred Cenote. During the spring and autumn equinoxes, sunlight creates a shadow pattern along the pyramid's staircase that resembles a serpent. Chichén Itzá is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Mexico's most famous archaeological attractions.

  • Location: Yucatán Peninsula, Mexico
  • Built By: Maya civilization
  • Famous Monument: El Castillo (Temple of Kukulcán)
  • Historical Role: Political and religious centre
  • Peak Period: Approximately 9th–12th centuries
  • Famous Structure: The Great Ball Court
  • UNESCO Status: World Heritage Site since 1988
  • Special Fact: Equinox sunlight creates a serpent-like shadow on the pyramid.

7. Colosseum

The Colosseum is an ancient Roman amphitheatre located in Rome, Italy. It was built during the Roman Empire and officially opened around 80 AD under Emperor Titus. The massive structure hosted public spectacles, including gladiator contests, animal hunts, and other events attended by thousands of spectators. It was designed with multiple levels of seating, arches, and passageways that allowed large crowds to enter and leave efficiently. The Colosseum was constructed mainly from stone and concrete and could accommodate tens of thousands of people. Although earthquakes and stone removal damaged parts of the building, much of it still stands today. It remains a powerful symbol of ancient Roman engineering, architecture, and history. The Colosseum is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Italy's most visited landmarks.

  • Location: Rome, Italy
  • Built By: Roman Empire
  • Opened: Around 80 AD
  • Type: Ancient amphitheatre
  • Material: Stone and concrete
  • Purpose: Public spectacles and gladiator contests
  • UNESCO Status: World Heritage Site since 1980
  • Special Fact: It is one of the largest surviving amphitheatres of the ancient Roman world.
